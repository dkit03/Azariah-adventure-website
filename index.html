Let's Keep Parang Alive!

History, Heritage, and Traditions

Adapted from the Parent & Teacher Guide (Spread 22) to help families, schools, and cultural lovers preserve the beautiful parang heritage of Trinidad & Tobago.

👑

The Queen of Parang: Daisy Voisin

Born in 1924, Daisy Voisin was the beloved matriarch of Trinidad parang. Based in Princes Town, she founded La Divina Pastora in 1971 and carried parang into the hearts of the nation and the world.

  • 💐 Always sang holding a fresh bouquet of yellow flowers
  • 🤍 Wore elegant white Caribbean dresses
  • 🎶 Composed "Alegria, Alegria", "Hurrah Hurrah", and "Sereno"
  • 🏅 Awarded the Hummingbird Medal (Gold) for Culture
🌴

The Roots of Parranda

Parang originated from the Spanish word parranda, meaning a spree or merrymaking. It was brought to Trinidad centuries ago by Spanish settlers, Venezuelan cocoa panyol workers, and indigenous Amerindian peoples.

  • 🚪 Paranderos visit neighborhood houses late at night
  • 🗣️ Traditional lyrics are sung in sweet Spanish patois
  • ⛪ Songs tell the sacred nativity story & festive cheer
  • 🤝 Promotes unity, community sharing, and hospitality
🪕

The Parang Instruments

A true parang band relies on acoustic percussion and stringed instruments working in tight polyrhythmic harmony:

  • 🪕 Cuatro: 4 nylon strings delivering the fast strum
  • 🥥 Chac-Chacs: Calabash maracas giving the crisp drive
  • 📦 Box Bass: Wooden box bass with resonant acoustic punch
  • 🪵 Toc-Toc: Resonant wooden clack block
  • 🎸 Guitar & Mandolin: Melodic counterpoint & sweetness
